3War-room signals as graphs: frequency, RoCoF, time error, PRC, inertia, voltage by bus, reactive power
Graphs, not ticking numbers
Every signal is a line that draws across the day and clears at midnight. The current value sits small beside each graph. Transformer loading stays only as board colour, and the "highest transformer load" number is gone.
Frequency, and what leads it
A scripted unit trip at 18:20 shows the chain. RoCoF spikes first, frequency falls to about 59.87 Hz, PRC drops as reserve is used, and time error steps. Inertia is lowest at midday, when solar displaces spinning units. PRC and inertia thresholds are marked UNVERIFIED until checked against ERCOT documents.
Voltage everywhere
Voltage by bus shows all 45 transformer buses against the 0.95–1.05 band, in feeder order. Mesquite Run rises at midday on solar backfeed and sags in the evening. Reactive power shows demand, the capacitor bank, our inverters' VAr support and the reserve left. 3b adds a bus × time heat strip.

2The heartbeat is fleet charge: batteries fill with excess solar and empty into the evening peak
One beat per day
The main rhythm is the net charge of all our batteries. It rises overnight on cheap power, climbs fast when rooftop solar passes household load, then empties into the evening price rise. It never dips below the 20% member reserve, which is drawn as a dotted floor. One day lasts 20 s, and the curve clears and redraws itself every midnight.
Overlay: load, solar, excess
Household load is a solid line and solar is dashed. The pale band where solar runs above load is labelled excess solar: the energy the batteries soak up. All three are scripted (ASSUMPTION) until the replays carry real series.
On the board
Pulses run along the lines at a rate set by fleet power. When the fleet charges they travel out from the substation, and when it discharges they run back in. Battery homes darken as they fill and swell on the beat. Stressed transformers still beat hardest. The previous direction is kept in v1.
