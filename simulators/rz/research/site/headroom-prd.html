Headroom

A feeder-aware orchestrator and what-if lab for a fleet of Base home batteries

ERCOT dispatches Base's fleet as one number per load zone and declines in writing to enforce distribution limits, so what the fleet can reliably deliver is decided at the feeder. Headroom proves it: real ERCOT prices, NREL's synthetic north-Austin feeders, every battery simulated with its own firmware rules, and an orchestrator made of real processes on a real bus that we kill, partition and hijack on camera.

1 · Summary

One number per load zone, 379 transformers on the street

  • Inputs: real ERCOT prices and NREL's synthetic north-Austin feeders (SMART-DS).
  • world-sim: every battery is its own device, with firmware rules, a radio link, a home and a service transformer.
  • Orchestrator: real OS processes on a real message bus, splitting the Markets desk's 5-minute base point across the fleet.
  • Test harness: kills those processes, cuts their network and steals their keys.
  • Detectors: physics-based, scored against ground truth they never see.

A controller that knows transformer and feeder headroom, and fences its own failures at the battery, keeps its commitments. A load-zone controller keeps them only by overloading transformers. We measure how many batteries a real-looking feeder hosts under each controller, what feeder awareness costs in MW and dollars, and how much harm a failure or attacker does before it is caught.

Binding conventions
  • Sign: battery p_kw > 0 is discharge/export; home load > 0 is consumption; p_site_kw = p_home − p_batt, > 0 is import.
  • Units are in field names: kW for devices, transformers, feeders; MW for partitions, system, market.
  • Time: t_ms is int64 UTC ms in sim time, labelled by interval start.
  • Tags: SOURCED cited fact · DERIVED computed, method given · ASSUMPTION our choice and a config knob · UNVERIFIED carried from research.

Full source: PRD.md in the project folder. Depth lives in design/round1/: world-sim (WS), orchestrator (OR), adversary-and-observability (AO), data-ingest (DI), ui-scenario-studio (UI), critique-judge (CJ), critique-integration (CI).

2 · Problem

ERCOT will not enforce the limit that matters

Base bids each load-zone partition into SCED as one ADER, dispatched using load-zone shift factors. The ADER governing document says distribution limits will not be explicitly enforced when ERCOT awards or dispatches it. Base's hardware sharpens the gap:

  • A 20 kW Core charging flat out is about 80% of a 25 kVA transformer.
  • Base's Houston partition swung its charging from −15.9 to −45.8 MW in 10–15 minutes.
  • Homebuilder deals put a battery in nearly every home on a feeder.

Base's deployment engineer asked exactly this: how each extra battery affects a stressed feeder, and how the fleet would behave if the controller knew feeder capacity.

  • Orchestration (primary track). The independent things are batteries, controller processes, and several principals (Markets desk, utility programs, each member's 20% floor). Physical pieces fail (links, feeders, lying devices) and so do ours (workers, leaders, the bus). Base's postings ask for controls for concentrations of aggregated batteries at distribution voltages and for automated fault detection, isolation and recovery.
  • Open Grid Data (second track). The simulator runs on ERCOT's own files (15-minute SPP, frequency-events workbook NP12-261-M, ADER workbooks) and yields two findings most readers miss (§9.5).
  • What Base lacks: no outage simulator, and grid-load math that doesn't teach its software to act better. Headroom is "a place to play the what-ifs", and its dispatch policy is a pluggable function, so Base's own black box can run the same scenarios.

Headless twin. The same code runs in one process on an in-memory bus with the same subjects, codecs and ACLs. It serves sweeps, fast-forward, the A/B baseline arm, CI and every determinism claim. Only one live run uses NATS at a time; A/B comparisons play two run logs synced on t_ms.

One SCED interval

  1. sim reads state_at(t), applies overlays, publishes exo.price (truth) and ctl.exo.price (the controller's view).
  2. orch-desk publishes ctl.basepoint.<partition_id>.
  3. orch-leader ramps the target, applies a PI correction with the reflex excluded, clips to the envelope, publishes ctl.plan before any command, then one ctl.budget.<shard_id> per shard.
  4. Workers holding shard leases split each budget across transformers and devices and publish signed ctl.cmd.<shard_id> (only entries that moved > deadband, plus renewals).
  5. sim's comms model delivers each command late, never, or twice.
  6. Devices apply §7.5; physics produces truth; meters sample it.
  7. Telemetry returns on sim.tele; workers update belief and send ctl.shardenv; the leader publishes ctl.envelope every 2 s and ctl.tracking every interval.
  8. obs-detect audits ctl.cmd.> against ctl.plan and asks ctl.control to mitigate; obs-score joins truth to alerts. JetStream plus the truth log form the run log.

6.1 world-sim (sim/)

Owns the clock and seeded RNG streams, homes, devices, comms and grid, the §7.5 acceptance rules, the truth/claim/meter split, the fault router's sim executor, the adversary, the truth log, and the hosting sweep harness (with orch/core as the policy under test).

  • Tick (1 s): exogenous inputs and due faults → deliver commands from the comms timing wheel → acceptance rules → home load → device update (mode, release, ramp, SoC, expiry) → np.bincount buckets per transformer and feeder → referee when due → tier bookkeeping → telemetry → meters → publish, latest-wins.
  • RNG: one SeedSequence spawns placement, load_noise, comms, device_delay, device_faults, adversary.
  • Device modes (highest priority first): REMOTE_DISABLED > FAULT_THERMAL > BACKUP_ISLANDED / OVERLOAD_RETRY > COMMS_LOST (label only) > STORM_HOLD > GRID_DISPATCH > GRID_IDLE.

  • Grid: spine uses a bucket model S_xfmr = Σ(p_home − p_batt) (home PF 0.95, battery PF 1.0), labelled fidelity:"bucket"; OpenDSS AC referee is L-01. A violation counts only if absent from, or worse than, a cached zero-battery twin.

6.2 Orchestrator (orch/core, orch/svc)

Desk stub produces base points; leader does tracking, envelope, derate ladder, plans and budgets; coordinator owns the shard map, leases, epochs and key rotation; workers keep belief, compute bottom-up envelopes, split top-down and sign commands. The control API executes mitigations (obs owns scores; orch owns enforcement and the commitment formula).

  • Device envelope: eligible = fresh ∧ not quarantined ∧ mode ∈ {GRID_DISPATCH, GRID_IDLE} ∧ energized. hi = min(P_rated, ramp, (soc − floor)·E/h); lo = −min(P_rated, ramp, (soc_max − soc)·E/(η·h)). The flip lock clamps both to the current side of zero.
  • Bottom-up envelope: transformer LO_k = max(Σlo, H_k − κ·S_k), HI_k = min(Σhi, H_k + S_k); feeder LO_f = max(ΣLO_k, L_f − (1−m)·F_f), HI_f = min(ΣHI_k, L_f + X_f); partition = Σ over energized feeders. LO_k > 0 means batteries must discharge to protect the transformer; LO > HI emits BUDGET_INFEASIBLE.
  • Top-down split: the same code at every level; clip to [LO, HI], bisect θ over stress-tilted weights. Stressed feeders discharge first and charge last. O(30·N).
  • Allocator arms (same base-point shape): naive flat pro-rata, delay off; naive_jitter flat, delay on; feeder_aware hierarchical split + rebound governor, delay on.
  • Tracker: target ramps linearly between base points over 300 s; P_meas excludes the reflex; PI clipped to the envelope with integrator freeze.
  • Belief: FRESH ≤10 s; SUSPECT 10–180 s (stop renewing, believe last command until expiry, then 0, start a same-feeder replacement at expiry); STALE >180 s; re-admit after 60 s fresh.
  • Commitment: firm = α·deliverable; at_risk = max(0, committed − firm). Derate ladder: re-split within feeders → move budget across feeders → spend tolerance while |e| < 0.5·tol → lower hsl_mw → COMMIT_DERATE.
  • Leases and keys: a new owner loads devstate.<shard> (≤10 s old) and resends the full command set at epoch+1. REVOKE_CREDENTIAL = epoch+1 → ctl.keys → full resend.
  • Governors: rebound caps charging ramp at 5% of F_f per minute; restoration holds 300 s, then caps recharge at (1−m)·F_f − CLPU·L_f (CLPU 2.0 for 15 min) through a token bucket.
  • Desk stub (synthetic, labelled): discharge follows Base's four published points (22:00 0.258, 22:05 0.591, 22:10 0.923, 22:15 0.996 pu of 46.9 MW), rising from 0 at 21:00, holding to 22:30, ramping to 0 over 15 min. 6.3 Scenario engine and adversary (scenario/)

  • The DSL in two forms (§7.10), the validator, the runner (each event gets an RNG seeded with hash(seed, event.id)), and one fault router with four executors: sim (grid, comms, device, member, weather), feed (overlays), ctl (supervisor, fault proxy, principal requests), obs (pipeline faults).
  • Validation: schema → semantics (IDs exist, power ≤ class rating, window exists, cyber events hold a granted capability, kind in /faults/manifest) → a 10-sim-minute headless dry run returning derived{devices_in_target, est_swing_mw}. Errors are JSON pointers with allowed ranges.
  • Spine adversary (scripted): credential_compromise grants a stolen shard key; setpoint_override publishes real signed cmd.v1 at the current epoch, seq = last_seen + 10⁶, p_kw = −P_max for every device, start_at_ms = now. Trigger: price ≥ trailing-day p95 for 300 s, or a fixed at_min.
  • Fog of war: the adversary sees only its foothold's claims, its own reject rate, and public ctl.exo fields.
  • Adaptive adversary (L-21): DORMANT → RECON → STRIKE or CREEP → EVADE; creep probes start at 20% bias and halve after each loss. Decision period 60 s; evade when quarantine rate > 0.3 or reject rate > 0.5 within 60 s.
